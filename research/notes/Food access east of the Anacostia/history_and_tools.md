# Food access east of the Anacostia: grocery-gap history, existing tools, and SMS feasibility (research notes, as of 2026-10-10)

Notes cover (a) the history of the grocery gap in DC Wards 7 and 8 from 2000 to 2026, (b) existing food-finder and food-desert mapping tools and where they fall short, and (c) facts needed to answer judges' questions about running the food finder over real SMS. Every claim has a source in the form [Publisher, title or description, date](URL). Where a date could not be confirmed, the citation says so. "Accessed Oct 2026" means a live web page read during this research, using its current content.

## (a1) Full-service grocery openings and closings east of the river, 2000–2026

### Takeaway
East of the river has had very few full-service grocery stores for the whole period. Only two large new stores opened there in about 20 years: the Giant at Camp Simms (December 2007, Ward 8) and the Lidl at Skyland (September 2022, Ward 7). Other attempts failed or stalled: Good Food Markets in Bellevue (opened 2021, closed in about a year), the Capitol Gateway site in Ward 7 (Walmart left in 2016, then a Giant deal fell apart by 2026), and St. Elizabeths East (a grocer is "potential" only). The latest count from D.C. Hunger Solutions (Nov 2025) is 3 stores in Ward 7 and 4 in Ward 8, against 17 in Ward 3 and 80 citywide. Ward 8's count includes stores in Navy Yard, which is west of the river.

### Cited Findings

**Timeline**
- **Dec 6, 2007: Giant opens at Camp Simms (Ward 8).** Mayor Fenty opened the store on the former National Guard site known as Camp Simms, along Alabama Ave SE. It was a 63,000 sq ft "Super Giant," Giant's largest store in DC, built with a $12M Giant investment and employing about 150 people. The press release called it "the first grocery store built in Ward 8 in decades." Developers were William C. Smith Cos. and East of the River CDC, as part of the Shops at Park Village. — [DMPED press advisory, "Fenty, Giant Open First New Grocery Store in Ward 8," Dec 6, 2007](https://dmped.dc.gov/release/fenty-giant-open-first-new-grocery-store-ward-8)
- The Camp Simms Giant is at 1535 Alabama Ave SE. — [NBC Washington, Sept 3, 2023](https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/local/giant-to-remove-brand-name-items-from-southeast-dc-store-shelves/3415999/). Another source gives 1551 Alabama Ave SE for the development: [New Markets Tax Credit Coalition project page, undated](https://nmtccoalition.org/project/shops-at-park-village/).
- **2016: Walmart pulls out of Capitol Gateway (East Capitol St & 58th St NE, Ward 7).** Walmart had been the planned anchor on the 11-acre site and withdrew in 2016. — [DCist, Jan 28, 2022](https://dcist.com/story/22/01/28/bowser-plans-to-use-eminent-domain-to-bring-grocery-store-to-long-vacant-lot-east-of-the-anacostia-river/). Walmart had also been slated to anchor Skyland before backing out. — [Washington Informer, "Ward 7 celebrates Lidl grocery store coming to Skyland Town Center," c. 2019–2022; exact date not verified](https://www.washingtoninformer.com/ward-7-celebrates-lidl-grocery-store-coming-to-skyland-town-center/)
- **2017: Safeway's Ward 7 stores under scrutiny.** Ward 7's two Safeways were the Minnesota Ave NE store (East River Park/Plaza) and the Alabama Ave SE store (Good Hope Plaza). In 2017 Councilmember Vincent Gray led surprise inspections over conditions there. Safeway said it had no plans to close either store. — [Washington Informer, "Gray leads surprise inspections at Ward 7 Safeway stores," 2017](https://washingtoninformer.com/gray-leads-surprise-inspections-at-ward-7-safeway-stores/); [Washingtonian, "Safeway is super cleaning its east of the river stores," Aug 25, 2017](https://washingtonian.com/2017/08/25/safeway-is-super-cleaning-its-east-of-the-river-stores/)
- **May 2019: Lidl at Skyland announced.** Mayor Bowser, Rappaport and WC Smith said Lidl would open its first DC store at Skyland Town Center (Good Hope Rd / Naylor Rd / Alabama Ave SE). — [Office of the Mayor, "Mayor Bowser announces Lidl grocery to open at Skyland Town Center in Ward 7," 2019](https://mayor.dc.gov/release/mayor-bowser-announces-lidl-grocery-open-skyland-town-center-ward-7)
- **Nov 2021: Good Food Markets opens in Bellevue (4001 South Capitol St SW, Ward 8).** DCist called it Ward 8's second full-service grocery store. — [DCist/WAMU, Nov 13, 2021](https://dcist.com/story/21/11/13/ward-8-gets-a-second-supermarket/)
  - The launch was backed by an $880,000 Neighborhood Prosperity Fund grant to South Capitol Improvement, LLC. — [Washington Informer, May 3, 2023](https://www.washingtoninformer.com/litigation-stalls-replacement-for-fresh-food-grocer-anc-commissioner-says/)
- **Jan–Jun 2022: Capitol Gateway eminent domain and a tentative Giant deal.** In January 2022 Bowser asked the Council to authorize eminent domain, warning the lot could otherwise sit vacant until at least 2037. The Council approved in February 2022. Giant then signed a tentative deal for a store of about 55K sq ft. — [DCist, Jan 28, 2022](https://dcist.com/story/22/01/28/bowser-plans-to-use-eminent-domain-to-bring-grocery-store-to-long-vacant-lot-east-of-the-anacostia-river/); [DCist, June 16, 2022](https://dcist.com/story/22/06/16/dc-giant-signs-deal-grocery-store-ward7/); [WTOP, June 2022](https://wtop.com/dc/2022/06/bowser-dc-to-use-eminent-domain-to-bring-a-new-giant-food-store-to-ward-7/); [Bisnow, 2022](https://www.bisnow.com/washington-dc/news/retail/giant-signaling-it-will-open-in-ward-7-as-bowser-pushes-eminent-domain-case-113444)
- **Sept 27, 2022: Lidl opens at Skyland Town Center (Ward 7).** It was Lidl's first DC store. DMPED called it "the first full-service grocery store to open East of the River in more than a decade," and Bowser said it accomplished "something that hasn't been done in 15 years." The release said 45 jobs, that Skyland was financed with Tax Increment Financing, and that the city had invested "$73 million in the Food Access Fund." — [DMPED press release, Sept 27, 2022](https://dmped.dc.gov/release/mayor-bowser-celebrates-grand-opening-dc%E2%80%99s-first-lidl-food-market)
  - Conflicting details: WTOP reported a Sept 27 ribbon-cutting, a Sept 28 grand opening, and "more than 60 jobs" at $16.50/hr starting pay. — [WTOP, Sept 2022](https://new.wtop.com/business-finance/2022/09/lidls-long-awaited-southeast-dc-grocery-store-opens-this-week/). Fox 5 also covered the opening. — [Fox 5 DC, Sept 2022](https://www.fox5dc.com/news/lidl-open-new-grocery-store-southeast-d-c-ward-7-ward-8-anacostia)
- **About late 2022: Good Food Markets Bellevue closes.** The store stopped selling fresh produce "a little over a year" after opening. A DMPED spokesperson said the chain closed its Bellevue and Ward 5 stores "in response to the pandemic." As of May 2023, the owner (Michaels Development) would not take new grocer bids until a $21,000 utility bill was settled. DMPED was finalizing a deal with an unnamed grocer. — [Washington Informer, May 3, 2023](https://www.washingtoninformer.com/litigation-stalls-replacement-for-fresh-food-grocer-anc-commissioner-says/)
- **Aug–Sept 2023: Camp Simms Giant under pressure from theft.** Councilmember Trayon White warned in August 2023 that shoplifting could force the store to close. Giant removed some national-brand health and beauty items, added receipt checks at the exit, and said it had no current plans to close. NBC described it as "one of the only full-service grocery stores east of the Anacostia River in Ward 8." The nearest other grocery was a Safeway about two miles away in Ward 7. — [NBC Washington, Sept 3, 2023](https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/local/giant-to-remove-brand-name-items-from-southeast-dc-store-shelves/3415999/)
- **Sept 27, 2025: Marion Barry Avenue Market & Cafe opens at 1303 Marion Barry Ave SE (Anacostia, Ward 8).** The 4,500 sq ft grocery, kitchen and restaurant is run by the nonprofit Dreaming Out Loud and carries more than 900 items. Funders included DMPED's Food Access Fund and José Andrés's Longer Tables Fund; no dollar amounts were published. Bradshaw said the market "can't compete with those big box retailers." — [WTOP, Oct 2, 2025](https://wtop.com/dc/2025/10/new-grocery-opens-in-anacostia-providing-more-options-for-ward-8-residents/); [Washingtonian, Oct 10, 2025](https://washingtonian.com/2025/10/10/new-anacostia-market-is-a-dream-come-true-for-community/). Civil Eats profiled it in May 2026 but the page could not be fetched (403). — [Civil Eats, May 13, 2026](https://civileats.com/2026/05/13/in-d-c-an-innovative-neighborhood-grocery-store/)
  - Washingtonian reported that before the market opened, Ward 8 had only one full-service grocery store for about 86,000 residents. — [Washingtonian, Oct 10, 2025](https://washingtonian.com/2025/10/10/new-anacostia-market-is-a-dream-come-true-for-community/)
- **May 16, 2026: Safeway at Hechinger Mall closes.** The store (Maryland Ave & Benning Rd NE) closed after nearly 40 years. Safeway cited the end of its lease. — [Fast Company, 2026 Safeway closures list](https://www.fastcompany.com/91590032/safeway-stores-closing-2026-locations-list); [Fox 5 DC, 2026](https://www.fox5dc.com/news/safeway-northeast-dc-set-close). No 2026 reporting was found on closures or renovations at the two Ward 7 Safeways (Minnesota Ave NE and Alabama Ave SE).
- **Sept 17, 2026: Capitol Gateway status.** Deputy Mayor Nina Albert said the District "now own[s] the land, free and clear" after a court ruling. The preliminary Giant deal "has fallen apart." The city is "back out into the market, trying to look for a new operator and grocer," is targeting chains that run smaller stores, and has about $27M budgeted for land and improvements. Disputes over price and proceeds continue, and the project will likely fall to the next mayor. Ward 7 residents still drive to Maryland or rely on delivery. — [The 51st (Alex Koma), Sept 17, 2026](https://51st.news/capitol-gateway-marketplace-grocery-store-ward-7/)
- **St. Elizabeths East (Ward 8, Congress Heights): grocer is "potential" only.** Parcels 7, 8 and 9 were awarded to St. Elizabeths Legacy Partners in December 2022. DMPED lists a "Potential Grocery Store" among planned features. — [DMPED, St. Elizabeths East Campus Redevelopment page, accessed Oct 2026](https://dmped.dc.gov/page/st-elizabeths-east-campus-redevelopment)
  - Parcel 15 plans include about 30,000 sq ft of commercial space with a grocer "envisioned." — [DCist, Mar 21, 2023](https://dcist.com/story/23/03/21/st-elizabeths-redevelopment-newest-plans/)
  - The FY2026 DC budget includes $15M for St. Elizabeths site improvements and $20M for Hill East infrastructure, both described as grocery and food-retail investment opportunities. — [DC Food Policy Council, "What is in the FY2026 District Budget for the DC Food System?", Aug 6, 2025](https://dcfoodpolicy.org/2025/08/06/what-is-in-the-fy2026-district-budget-for-dc-food-system/)

**Store counts over time (D.C. Hunger Solutions "grocery gap" series, plus Office of Planning)**
- **Nov 19, 2025 (latest).** *Minding the Grocery Gap in D.C.: A 2025 Update* counts 80 full-service stores citywide (up from 76): **Ward 7 = 3, Ward 8 = 4**, Ward 3 = 17. More than 140,000 DC residents rely on SNAP. — [D.C. Hunger Solutions press release, Nov 19, 2025](https://www.dchunger.org/uncategorized/residents-in-wards-7-and-8-still-have-limited-access-to-grocery-stores-d-c-hunger-solutions-report-finds/)
- **April 2024.** Office of Planning analysis using WDCEP data and the ABCA definition of a full-service grocery: 64 full-service grocery stores citywide, "yet only 4 for the 158,000 residents living east of the Anacostia River." — [DC Food Policy Council & Office of Planning, *Food Access & Food Economy Report FY2024*, transmitted to Council Sept 9, 2025 (PDF), p.11 and note xix](https://dcfoodpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FY24-Food-Access-Food-Economy-Report.pdf)
- **Earlier counts** (taken from search excerpts of DCHS pages; the page-to-year mapping was not individually verified):
  - 2010: 4 in Ward 7 and 3 in Ward 8.
  - Spring 2016: 2 in Ward 7 and 1 in Ward 8, out of 49 citywide. — [D.C. Hunger Solutions, "Grocery Store Gap Has Widened Since 2010," c. 2016–17](https://dchunger.org/?p=2884)
  - 2023 update: 4 and 3. 2024: 3 and 3. — [D.C. Hunger Solutions, "Still Too Few Grocery Stores… East of the River"](https://dchunger.org/?p=3863); [DCHS, 2021 report PDF "Still Minding the Grocery Gap"](https://www.dchunger.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/DCHS-Report_Still-Minding-the-Grocery-Gap_2021.pdf)
  - DCHS itself cautions that the 2010 and 2016 counts are hard to compare because of the 2012 ward boundary changes and a change in how "full-service" was applied (same source).
- **Why the counts disagree: redistricting.** After the 2022 redistricting, Ward 8 crossed the river and picked up a Harris Teeter and a Whole Foods (Navy Yard). FRAC argued these stores are not accessible to most Ward 8 residents. — [FRAC blog, "Disparities to Grocery Store Accessibility Remain Amid Redistricting," c. 2022; date not verified](https://frac.org/blog/disparities-to-grocery-store-accessibility-remain)
  - A 2025 piece counted 4 full-service stores for about 86,500 Ward 8 residents, 2 of them in Navy Yard. — [The 51st, "Ask a D.C. Native: What should I know about Ward 8?", 2025](https://51st.news/ask-dc-native-ward8/)

**Other context**
- In 2017, 80% of DC's food deserts were in Wards 7 and 8 (DC Policy Center figure, as cited in 2022 Lidl coverage). — [Washington Informer, Lidl coverage, 2022; secondary citation of DC Policy Center, original not checked](https://www.washingtoninformer.com/ward-7-celebrates-lidl-grocery-store-coming-to-skyland-town-center/)

### Inferences
- The best one-line framing for the pitch is the April 2024 Office of Planning figure: 4 full-service stores for 158,000 residents east of the river, versus 64 citywide. It is a government number and is not distorted by the Navy Yard redistricting. (This is my framing.)
- Good Food Markets lasted about a year, the Camp Simms Giant came under theft pressure in 2023, and Capitol Gateway's Giant deal fell apart. Together these show that brick-and-mortar fixes are slow, fragile, and take years. That supports a tool that surfaces unmet need continuously between store openings.

### Gaps
- The exact closing date of Good Food Markets Bellevue was not found; sources say only "a little over a year" after its fall 2021 opening.
- No source confirmed whether a replacement grocer ever opened at 4001 South Capitol St SW after 2023.
- There is no clear 2000–2007 record of Ward 7/8 store closings (for example, older Safeway or Murry's closures). Not researched for lack of sources.
- WUSA9's 2026 article on the mayoral race grocery proposals returned 403 (see a3).

## (a2) Incentives and community food infrastructure (Food Access Fund, tax exemption, nonprofits)

### Takeaway
DC's main tools have been the Supermarket Tax Exemption Act of 2000, the Neighborhood Prosperity Fund, and the ARPA-funded Food Access Fund (FAF, FY21–FY23 rounds). FAF paid out "more than $23 million" for food businesses in low-access areas. With ARPA money gone at the end of 2024, the city's own FY2024 report called FAF's future "uncertain." Nonprofits fill much of the gap: Martha's Table at The Commons (2018), Dreaming Out Loud (since 2008), the Ward 8 Farmers Market (since 1999), Healthy Corner Stores, and Produce Plus.

### Cited Findings

**Policy and money**
- **Supermarket Tax Exemption Act of 2000.** D.C. Hunger Solutions asked in Nov 2025 that it be reformed by tying incentives to affordable pricing, fresh produce, and SNAP/WIC acceptance. — [DCHS, Nov 19, 2025](https://www.dchunger.org/uncategorized/residents-in-wards-7-and-8-still-have-limited-access-to-grocery-stores-d-c-hunger-solutions-report-finds/)
  - DMPED in 2022 cited "changes to the Supermarket Tax Incentive" and the Neighborhood Prosperity Fund as tools. — [DMPED, Sept 27, 2022](https://dmped.dc.gov/release/mayor-bowser-celebrates-grand-opening-dc%E2%80%99s-first-lidl-food-market)
- **Food Access Fund (DMPED) design.** Grants paid for tenant improvements for food businesses with at least 2 years of operations that open a new location in a low-food-access area, with priority for Wards 7 and 8. — [DMPED, FY2021 FAF NOFA (PDF)](https://dmped.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/dmped/publication/attachments/FY2021%20Food%20Access%20Fund.pdf)
- **FAF funding rounds.**
  - FY21: up to $23,647,000 available; about $9M announced.
  - FY22: up to $7M, rolling applications. DMPED projected 6–8 new access points, putting 95–99% of Ward 7/8 residents within a mile of one. — [FY22 FAF NOFA, DC Office of Community Affairs](https://communityaffairs.dc.gov/sites/moca/files/dc/sites/moca/publication/attachments/FY22%20Food%20Access%20Fund%20Grant_NOFA.pdf)
  - FY23 (Round 4): application window Dec 12, 2022–Jan 12, 2023. The program page says $5M, but the amended NOFA says $10,688,513, which conflicts. — [DMPED FAF page (marked CLOSED)](https://dmped.dc.gov/node/1546801); [FY23 FAF NOFA amended](https://communityaffairs.dc.gov/sites/moca/files/dc/sites/moca/publication/attachments/FY23%20Food%20Access%20Fund%20Grant_NOFA%20%28AMENDED%29.pdf)
  - FY21 awardees east of the river included DCity Smokehouse (Anacostia), Fresh Food Factory (Kenilworth), and HalfSmoke (Skyland and Minnesota Ave). — [DC Food Policy Council meeting deck, Dec 2021](https://dcfoodpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/december-2021-fpc-meeting-.pdf)
- **FAF totals and wind-down.**
  - The FY2024 report says "more than $23 million distributed through the Food Access Fund, which was funded by American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds," and that "With ARPA funds expiring at the end of 2024 … the future of the Food Access Fund is uncertain." It also lists Nourish DC: more than $17M in loans, grants and technical assistance to food businesses in Wards 5, 7 and 8 over three years. — [Food Access & Food Economy Report FY2024, Sept 9, 2025](https://dcfoodpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FY24-Food-Access-Food-Economy-Report.pdf)
  - In contrast, DMPED's 2022 release said the city "invested $73 million in the Food Access Fund." "Invested" vs "distributed" may explain the gap. — [DMPED, Sept 27, 2022](https://dmped.dc.gov/release/mayor-bowser-celebrates-grand-opening-dc%E2%80%99s-first-lidl-food-market)
  - The FY2026 food-budget summary does not mention FAF at all. — [DC Food Policy Council, Aug 6, 2025](https://dcfoodpolicy.org/2025/08/06/what-is-in-the-fy2026-district-budget-for-dc-food-system/)
- **Capitol Gateway land purchase.** 2022 reporting said FAF money would pay about $20M toward the eminent-domain purchase. — [Bisnow, 2022](https://www.bisnow.com/news/washington-dc/mixed-use/we-will-not-stop-bowser-calls-for-eminent-domain-use-to-secure-grocery-store-in-ward-7-111664)

**Community infrastructure**
- **Martha's Table at The Commons at Stanton Square.** The nonprofit moved to 2375 Elvans Rd SE (Ward 8) with a grand opening June 23, 2018; the campus was built with Community of Hope and the Horning family. Early on it ran a free lobby market with produce and staples. — [Martha's Table blog, June 4, 2018](https://marthastable.org/blog/join-us-at-our-grand-opening/); [Community of Hope, 1-year anniversary release, June 2019](https://www.communityofhopedc.org/the-commons-at-stanton-square-celebrates-one-year-anniversary-with-special-community-event-on-friday-june-28-2019/)
  - FY2026 budget: Joyful Food Markets (Martha's Table) $1.77M, a $50K cut. — [DC Food Policy Council, Aug 6, 2025](https://dcfoodpolicy.org/2025/08/06/what-is-in-the-fy2026-district-budget-for-dc-food-system/)
- **Dreaming Out Loud.** Founded in 2008 by Chris Bradshaw for food equity east of the river. It now runs the Marion Barry Avenue Market (opened Sept 27, 2025). — [Washingtonian, Oct 10, 2025](https://washingtonian.com/2025/10/10/new-anacostia-market-is-a-dream-come-true-for-community/)
- **Ward 8 Farmers Market.** Residents have had access to it since 1999. — [Awesome Foundation project page, undated](https://www.awesomefoundation.org/nl/projects/43945-ward-8-farmers-market). A 2012 public radio story credits John Gloster with helping set it up when "there was no real grocery store anywhere for miles around." — [WGBH/NPR, Oct 15, 2012](https://www.wgbh.org/news/2012-10-15/on-the-farmers-market-frontier-its-not-just-about-profit)
- **Healthy Corner Stores** (DC Health and DC Central Kitchen). In FY2023, healthy food sales rose 11.6%. The 33 stores offering SNAP matching served more than 15,000 customers. Citywide there are 57 Healthy Corner Stores and 53 farmers markets. — [FY2024 Food Access & Food Economy Report, Sept 9, 2025](https://dcfoodpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FY24-Food-Access-Food-Economy-Report.pdf). FY2026 funding: $750K, flat. — [DC Food Policy Council, Aug 6, 2025](https://dcfoodpolicy.org/2025/08/06/what-is-in-the-fy2026-district-budget-for-dc-food-system/)
- **Produce Plus** (run by FRESHFARM). FY2026 funding was $2.05M, a $50K cut. — [DC Food Policy Council, Aug 6, 2025](https://dcfoodpolicy.org/2025/08/06/what-is-in-the-fy2026-district-budget-for-dc-food-system/)
  - A $600K increase in FY2025 cut the waitlist from 4,717 to 2,035 (per a search excerpt of Legal Aid DC's budget blog). — [Legal Aid DC, "DC's 2025 Budget," 2024](https://www.legalaiddc.org/blogs/dc-2025-budget-programs)
- **Community Grocery Co-op.** A resident-led co-op/buying club is working to build a store east of the river. — [Congress Heights on the Rise, "Registration open for community grocery co-op buying club," date not verified](https://congressheightsontherise.com/blog/registration-open-for-community-grocery-co-op-buying-club?format=amp)

### Inferences
- With ARPA-era money (FAF, Nourish DC) winding down and FY2026 and FY2027 budgets tight, the city needs cheaper ways to target scarce food-access dollars. A live demand map showing where people look for food and find nothing nearby is a targeting tool for exactly that. (This is my framing.)

### Gaps
- Final FAF award totals by ward and by year were not found.
- No FY2027 FAF line item was found.
- "Ward 8 Woods" and the "Community Purchasing Alliance" (named in the brief) were not found in any source tied to food access, so they were not included.

## (a3) 2025–2026 news: SNAP disruption, federal cuts, DC budget, hunger data, mayoral race

### Takeaway
2025–2026 made food access worse. The fall 2025 shutdown forced DC to plan to pay November SNAP with local money. H.R. 1 (2025) is cutting SNAP eligibility and shifting costs to DC. DC repealed its local "Give SNAP a Raise" supplement in the FY2026 budget. Regional food insecurity hit a five-year high of 38% (CAFB, Sept 2026). The grocery gap is now a 2026 mayoral campaign issue.

### Cited Findings

**Fall 2025 shutdown**
- DC officials said they would use local contingency funds so residents would get November 2025 SNAP and WIC. Estimates were about $29M for SNAP and $1.5M for WIC. About 85,000 households (about 141,000 residents) receive SNAP each month. USDA said it would not use contingency funds or reimburse states. — [Axios DC, Oct 28, 2025](https://www.axios.com/local/washington-dc/2025/10/28/dc-snap-benefits-government-shutdown); [Office of the Mayor release, Oct/Nov 2025](https://mayor.dc.gov/release/mayor-bowser-announces-district-ensure-washingtonians-receive-november-snap-and-wic-benefits); [The 51st, Oct 2025](https://51st.news/dc-funding-snap-food-benefits/)
- CAFB's 2026 Hunger Report names "the fall 2025 federal government shutdown" as a driver of food insecurity. — [WJLA/7News, Sept 15, 2026](https://wjla.com/news/local/capital-area-food-bank-hunger-report-2026-insecurity-dmv-dc-virginia-maryland-prince-georges-county-arlington-household-finances-cost-of-living-federal-jobs-inflation-immigrant-families-economy)

**H.R. 1 (2025 reconciliation law) and SNAP**
- An estimated 17,000 DC SNAP participants are "at risk of losing access to SNAP" under H.R. 1. DC's FY2025 SNAP payment error rate was 18.66%, which could expose DC to "some of the steepest" penalties under the new cost-share. — [D.C. Hunger Solutions press release, June 25, 2026](https://www.dchunger.org/?p=4473)
- H.R. 1 adds stricter work requirements and removes exemptions for veterans, people experiencing homelessness, and former foster youth aged 24 and under. DCHS asked Congress to repeal these provisions. — [DCHS, Nov 19, 2025](https://www.dchunger.org/uncategorized/residents-in-wards-7-and-8-still-have-limited-access-to-grocery-stores-d-c-hunger-solutions-report-finds/)
- States' share of SNAP administrative costs rises from 50% to 75% starting FY2027 (Oct 1, 2026). Benefit cost-sharing tied to error rates begins FY2028. — [NCSL, "States Brace for SNAP Cost Shifts," 2025–26; from search excerpt](https://www.ncsl.org/resources/details/States-Brace-for-SNAP-Cost-Shifts)
  - DC budget materials reportedly list a $36M increase in SNAP administrative costs from federal changes (search excerpt only). — [Legal Aid DC, "DC's 2027 Budget: Back from the Brink," 2026](https://www.legalaiddc.org/blogs/dc-final-budget-2027)

**DC budget**
- The FY2026 Budget Support Act repealed the Give SNAP a Raise Amendment Act of 2022, DC's local SNAP supplement. — [DC Food Policy Council, Aug 6, 2025](https://dcfoodpolicy.org/2025/08/06/what-is-in-the-fy2026-district-budget-for-dc-food-system/)
- Background: Give SNAP a Raise was funded with $39M in FY2024. When the federal Emergency Allotments ended after the Feb 2023 issuance, average DC SNAP benefits fell 34%. SNAP supports "one in five District residents." — [FY2024 Food Access & Food Economy Report, Sept 9, 2025](https://dcfoodpolicy.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/FY24-Food-Access-Food-Economy-Report.pdf); [DCHS, "Commends decision to fund Give SNAP a Raise," 2023](https://www.dchunger.org/news-releases/commends-decision-fund-give-snap-raise/)
- FY2027 budget (passed July 2026): the Mayor proposed broad social-program cuts. The Council restored much of the funding, largely with one-time money. — [Legal Aid DC, 2026](https://www.legalaiddc.org/blogs/dc-final-budget-2027); [Street Sense Media, 2026](https://streetsensemedia.org/article/d-c-council-unanimously-passes-budget-finding-one-time-funds-for-social-services/)

## (b) Existing food-finder and food-desert tools, and what they cannot do

### Takeaway
DC's existing tools fall into two groups:
- **Finders for residents:** the CAFB map, the DC 211 Warmline, findhelp.org, and the Propel app. They need a smartphone or web browser, or a phone call during business hours. No DC-specific, year-round SMS food finder was found.

## (c1) Running it over real SMS: pricing, registration, timelines

### Takeaway
Running this for real is cheap but needs about 2–3 weeks of carrier registration lead time.
- **Cost:** Twilio charges $0.0083 per SMS segment each way plus carrier fees of roughly $0.0025–$0.005, and $1.15/month for a local number. A typical request (1 inbound text, 3-segment reply) costs about 5 cents. Telnyx comes in at about 3 cents.
- **Registration:** a local 10DLC number needs brand and campaign registration (about $4.50–$46 brand fee, $15 campaign vetting, $1.50–$10/month) with 10–15 day campaign reviews. A toll-free number needs verification (about 1–3 weeks). A short code costs about $1,000+/month and takes 6–10+ weeks.

- **Twilio:**
  - Inbound: $0.0083 + about $0.0025–$0.0035 ≈ $0.011–$0.012.
  - Outbound: 3 × ($0.0083 + $0.0035–$0.005) ≈ $0.035–$0.040.
  - Total ≈ **$0.046–$0.052 per request**.
  - 10,000 requests a month ≈ $460–$520 plus $1.15 for the number and $3–$10 for the campaign. One-time setup ≈ $19.50 (low-volume brand $4.50 + $15 vetting) to $61 (standard brand $46 + $15).
- **Telnyx:**
  - Total ≈ (0.004 + 0.0025–0.0035) + 3 × (0.004 + 0.0035–0.005) ≈ **$0.029–$0.035 per request**.

**Which number type to pick:**
- For a pilot: a toll-free number (verification, no 10DLC brand fees, $2.15/month on Twilio), or a local 10DLC number registered as a charity or low-volume campaign. Either is realistic within about 2–3 weeks.
- A short code (memorable, like 304-304) costs about $12K/year in rent and takes 2–3 months. It is a later step if the city adopts the line.
- Partnering with an existing nonprofit (CAFB, Martha's Table, Dreaming Out Loud) could qualify for the $3/month charity campaign tier, if that nonprofit registers as the brand.

**Segment math caveat:** the standard SMS segment limits (160 GSM-7 characters per single segment, 153 per segment when concatenated, and 70 or 67 for Unicode text, which accented Spanish characters can trigger) are general knowledge and not sourced here. Spanish replies using "á/é/ñ" may cost more segments. Option: send accent-free Spanish, or keep replies short.

## (c2) Privacy: phone numbers as PII and how to avoid storing them

### Takeaway
Twilio offers account-level Message Redaction:
- **Phone numbers:** the last 4 digits of user phone numbers are replaced with "XXXX" in Twilio's logs and APIs.
- **Message bodies:** text can be redacted too, and full data stays in production for no more than 24 hours.
- **Per message:** API flags let you obfuscate the number and discard the content.

The app itself can avoid storing numbers by keeping only coarse place, time and need category. Two caveats: Twilio's built-in STOP handling keeps opted-out numbers, and Twilio still retains data in limited-access compliance storage.

### Cited Findings
- **What redaction covers.** Message Redaction redacts phone numbers and message bodies independently. Phone-number redaction "replaces the last four digits of non-Twilio-owned phone numbers with XXXX."
- **Retention.** Twilio keeps production access to messaging data "for up to 24 hours," after which it is "retained in separate, limited-access storage for compliance purposes." Twilio keeps the full number "only as long as required to deliver the message"; downstream systems see only the redacted number.
- **Per-message controls.** `AddressRetention=obfuscate` and `ContentRetention=discard` can be set on each message. Access to Message Redaction must be requested and then enabled in Console.
- **Gotchas:**
  - Webhooks should use POST, because Twilio logs GET request parameters for up to 7 days.
  - Built-in STOP filtering saves opted-out numbers to the account's block list, which "could potentially violate the phone number redaction guarantee." If you turn it off, you must handle opt-outs yourself.
  - Redaction is incompatible with Sticky Sender, Scheduled SMS, Studio, Flex and Conversations.
- Sources for the four points above: [Twilio Docs, "Message Redaction for Programmable Messaging," accessed Oct 2026](https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/guides/privacy-message-redaction); [Twilio blog, "Message Redaction Supports Phone Number and Content Privacy for Sensitive Applications"](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/blog/protect-customer-privacy-with-redaction)
- **Twilio's own definition.** Twilio's glossary treats phone numbers as personally identifiable information. — [Twilio glossary, "What is PII"](https://twilio.com/docs/glossary/what-is-personally-identifiable-information-pii)
- **Why anonymity matters here.** Fear of immigration enforcement is causing some foreign-born residents to avoid food and health services. — [WJLA on CAFB Hunger Report 2026, Sept 15, 2026](https://wjla.com/news/local/capital-area-food-bank-hunger-report-2026-insecurity-dmv-dc-virginia-maryland-prince-georges-county-arlington-household-finances-cost-of-living-federal-jobs-inflation-immigrant-families-economy)
- **Texting is discreet.** A 211 director noted texters can delete the message (for example, from a partner checking their phone) and still get a reply. — [MyTwinTiers / 211 coverage, date not verified](https://digital-release.mytwintiers.com/?p=11641)

### Inferences
Suggested privacy architecture for the pitch (my design suggestion, not sourced):
- Turn on Twilio phone-number and body redaction and use POST webhooks.
- In the app, process each message in memory. Keep only the resolved area (ZIP or neighborhood centroid, never an exact address), a timestamp rounded to the hour, the need type, and whether a resource was found within X miles. Never store the phone number.
- If repeat-user counting is needed, use a salted hash that rotates daily, then discard it.
- Publish map cells only above a minimum count (for example, k ≥ 5) to prevent re-identification in low-density blocks.
- Handle STOP/HELP yourself so the opted-out list can be kept as salted hashes instead of raw numbers.

### Gaps
- No source was found on how long US wireless carriers keep SMS metadata or content. Judges may ask. Answer honestly that carriers keep their own records, which the service cannot control (unsourced).
- No DC-specific law on handling phone numbers by city-contracted SMS services was researched.

## (c3) Spanish-language need and the DC Language Access Act

### Takeaway
About 8.7% of DC households speak Spanish at home, and about 5.7% of residents aged 5+ (about 37,000 people) speak English less than "very well." Spanish is the top language among residents with limited English. Wards 7 and 8 have small Hispanic populations: about 5% and 4% in a 2025 commercial estimate, against 12% citywide. So Spanish support matters more for citywide scale-up and for regional immigrant households than for Ward 7/8 volume. DC's Language Access Act of 2004 requires agencies to translate vital documents for any language group that is 3% or 500 people (whichever is less) of the population they serve.

### Cited Findings
- **Limited English proficiency in DC.** 5.7% (37,257) of DC residents aged 5+ speak English less than "very well" (2016–2020 ACS 5-year). — [DC Health, "Language Access" deck (PDF), c. 2022](https://livingwell.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/livingwell/page_content/attachments/DC-Health-Language-Access.pdf)
- **Spanish at home.** 8.7% of DC households speak Spanish at home and 10.3% speak a language other than English. The same deck says about 1 in 10 residents has limited or no English, which conflicts with the 5.7% ACS figure (likely a different measure or base). — [DC Department of Parks & Recreation, Language Access Training deck, March 2025](https://dpr.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/dpr/page_content/attachments/DPR%20Language%20Access%20Training_March2025.pdf)
- **Agency encounters.** In FY18, DC agencies reported more than 180,000 encounters with residents with limited English, across 69 languages, with Spanish the most common. — [DC Office of Human Rights, Language Access Program data page](https://ohr.dc.gov/page/data-sources-demographic-data-districts-lep-population)
- **Older trend.** DC's limited-English share was 7.1% in 2000 and 4.6% in 2007, with Spanish the leading language. — [Brookings, Language access presentation, 2007/2008 (PDF)](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/0715_language_access_ppt.pdf)
- **Ward level (low confidence).** A Claritas 2025 estimate puts Hispanic/Latino residents at 5.14% of Ward 7 (3,831) and 3.81% of Ward 8 (2,789), versus 12.3% citywide. It is hosted on a DC Health Matters test subdomain. Hispanic ethnicity is not the same as speaking Spanish or having limited English. — [DC Health Matters (test site), Claritas 2025 demographic indicator](https://test-washingtondc.thehcn.net/demographicdata/index/view?id=1534)
- **Regional context.** 25% of DMV residents are foreign-born, and 40% of those were born in Latin America. — [WJLA on CAFB Hunger Report 2026, Sept 15, 2026](https://wjla.com/news/local/capital-area-food-bank-hunger-report-2026-insecurity-dmv-dc-virginia-maryland-prince-georges-county-arlington-household-finances-cost-of-living-federal-jobs-inflation-immigrant-families-economy)
- **DC Language Access Act of 2004.**
  - Covered agencies must provide written translations of vital documents for any limited-English language group that makes up 3% or 500 people (whichever is less) of the population they serve or encounter.
  - They must also collect annual data on the languages their customers speak.
  - Sources: [Language Access Act of 2004 text (via LEP.gov PDF)](https://www.lep.gov/sites/lep/files/resources/DCLanguageAccessActof2004.pdf); [D.C. Code § 2-1932 (FindLaw)](https://codes.findlaw.com/dc/division-i-government-of-district/dc-code-sect-2-1932/); [DC Office of Human Rights](https://ohr.dc.gov/node/1409991)

### Inferences
- **For judges:** Spanish keyword support (as USDA does with COMIDA) is cheap to add. It matches the Language Access Act's spirit if the city adopts the line. Note that any DC agency running it would be legally covered by the Act. Spanish demand will be lower in Wards 7 and 8 than citywide, and the demand map itself could show where Spanish requests come from.
